**Observed:** `[[0,0,"full"],[4.2,0.67,"full"],[8.4,1.35,"full"],[12.6,2.03,"full"],[16.8,2.71,"full"],[21,3.38,"full"]]` then `seconds per day (full): 6.20`. The same drift shows with the three pages open (day 4.87 after 30 s). Slow speed drifts in the same proportion.

**Expected:** `seconds per day (full)` between 4.90 and 5.10 (design "World rules": 5 s per day at full speed, 20 s per day while slow; WO-03 section 1: one 0.01-day step every 50 ms at full, every 200 ms at slow).

**File:** `Monitor/src/host/pacing.js` line 14: each step is a chained `setTimeout(..., FULL_MS)` scheduled after the previous callback returns, so every step adds the callback's own time plus the timer's latency; on Windows a 50 ms timeout fires at about 62 ms (the OS timer tick is 15.6 ms), which is exactly 6.2 s per day.

**Fix shape (no design choice involved):** pace from elapsed wall time, not from timer counts. On each timer callback compute the wall milliseconds since the last step was accounted for, run `Math.floor(elapsed / stepMs)` steps of `STEP_DAYS` (keep `0.01` per step so triggers land on the same ticks as the headless run), carry the remainder forward, and cap the catch-up at a sane number of steps per callback (for example 20) so a stalled process does not jump. `slow`, `paused`, `fast` (test builds) and `sync()` keep their meaning; `fast` may stay as it is. Keep determinism: the number of steps may vary with wall time, the content of each step may not.

**Also:** add a unit test in `Monitor/src/host/host.test.js` (or a new `Monitor/src/host/pacing.test.js`) that drives the pacer with a fake clock and asserts that 1,000 ms of wall time at full speed yields 20 steps (±1) and at slow speed 5 steps (±1).
